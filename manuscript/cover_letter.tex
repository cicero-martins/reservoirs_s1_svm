As instructed in your decision letter of 8 June 2026, we reference here the
prior manuscript number, \textbf{ENVSOFT-D-26-00848} (``Monitoring Reservoir
Surface and Storage Dynamics Using Sentinel-1 SAR and Machine Learning in
Google Earth Engine'').

Below, we detail point by point how the manuscript
has changed in response to your comments and those of both reviewers. The
paper has been substantially reframed rather than incrementally patched, so we
first summarize the reframing before addressing each comment individually.

\section*{Summary of the reframing}

Both reviewers and the handling editor converged on three concerns:
insufficient novelty relative to prior SAR-plus-machine-learning studies,
an unsubstantiated scalability claim, and an experimental setup validated on
too few reservoirs to support its conclusions. Rather than defend the
original framing, we changed what the paper is about.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
  \item \textbf{Novelty.} The manuscript's central claim is no longer that a
  GEE workflow using VV/VH Sentinel-1 and an SVM can monitor Sicilian
  reservoirs. It is that a reservoir's shoreline \textbf{area-to-perimeter
  ratio (A/P)} --- a length-scale computable from global databases before any
  image is processed --- predicts, a priori, the geometric ceiling on SAR
  classification skill (Introduction, Section~1; Section~3.6; Results,
  Section~4.1; Discussion, Section~5.2). This claim was already at 
  the previous paper, and it is the direction Reviewer~1 identified 
  as the paper's most interesting, under-exploited
  thread (their General Comment~3).
  \item \textbf{Scalability.} All manual training-sample delineation has been
  removed. Both classifiers are now trained automatically from global JRC
  Global Surface Water and ESA WorldCover masks (Section~3.3), and this fully
  automated pipeline is tested on \textbf{62 reference-quality-screened
  reservoirs across 33 countries and five continents} (Section~2.1), not on a
  single region.
  \item \textbf{Experimental setup and scope.} The manuscript no longer
  attempts to convert surface area to storage volume. That conversion
  depended on area--elevation--volume (AEV) curves built from historical
  design surveys, and, as Reviewer~1 rightly noted (specific comment~12), our
  attempt to justify replacing them while still relying on them was
  internally inconsistent. We now scope the paper to what it can support
  rigorously, the SAR-derived surface area itself, and leave the AEV/volume
  problem to future work building on this result (Discussion,
  Section~5.4). This change resolves the majority of the Results and
  Discussion issues raised by both reviewers (Reviewer~1 comments 12, 17--20;
  Reviewer~2 items E.4, E.5, F), since the disputed material no longer
  appears.
  \item \textbf{Validation.} The four Sicilian reservoirs are retained, but
  now as a high-resolution (3~m PlanetScope) near-truth check layered on top
  of the 62-reservoir global comparison against the JRC Global Surface Water
  record (Sections~2, 3.7), rather than as the study's only validation.
  \item \textbf{Baseline.} The single-polarisation baseline is now
  Otsu's~(1979) between-class-variance threshold, recomputed independently
  for every acquisition (Section~3.4.1), directly answering Reviewer~1's
  suggestion (specific comment~22) to compare against an established,
  well-characterised threshold method rather than an ad hoc cut, and it is
  compared under an identical pipeline to a dual-polarisation SVM that is
  likewise re-trained per scene (Section~3.4.2).
  \item \textbf{Cost.} A computational-cost audit (Earth Engine Compute Unit
  usage; Section~3.8, Section~4.4) now accompanies the accuracy comparison,
  answering Reviewer~1's question on this point directly.
\end{itemize}

We address every remaining comment individually below, organised by source
(Editor, Reviewer~1, Reviewer~2) and following each reviewer's own numbering.
Where a comment concerned material that no longer appears in the manuscript
(chiefly the storage-volume analysis and the multi-classifier comparison), we
say so explicitly rather than force an answer to a question that no longer
applies.

\section*{Response to the Editor}

\begin{resp}
  \item[Novelty, scalability, experimental setup.] Each is addressed
  structurally, not just rhetorically: novelty by re-centring the paper on
  the A/P mechanism (Sections~1, 3.6, 4.1, 5.2); scalability by removing
  manual training entirely (Section~3.3) and testing on 62 reservoirs on five
  continents instead of one region (Section~2.1); experimental setup by
  reorganising Sections~3--4 around the five workflow stages in
  the workflow figure (Figure~2, as requested by Reviewer~2, items D.1, E.1, E.2)
  and by removing the storage-volume scope that generated most of the
  original Results/Discussion difficulties.
\end{resp}

\section*{Response to Reviewer 1}

\subsection*{General comments}

\begin{resp}
  \item[1. Workflow is scalable, the model is not.] Agreed, and fixed at the
  root: the model itself no longer requires manual, per-location training.
  Both detectors are trained automatically from global JRC/WorldCover masks
  (Section~3.3), and we are explicit that only the \emph{static}, a-priori A/P
  is shown to generalise; we do \emph{not} claim a dynamic, per-scene
  switching rule is scalable, because we could not establish that one
  generalises (Discussion, Section~5.4, final two paragraphs).
  \item[2. Only four of many reservoirs validated.] The primary comparison
  now runs on 62 reservoirs referenced against JRC (Sections~2.1, 3.7.1,
  4.1--4.2); the four Sicilian reservoirs remain as a secondary,
  high-resolution near-truth layer (Sections~2.2, 3.7.2, 4.3), not the sole
  validation.
  \item[3. Novelty vs.\ widely applied SAR+SVM approaches.] We agree with the
  reviewer's own suggestion: the paper's contribution is now the
  quantification of morphometric (A/P) control over SAR classification
  performance (Sections~1, 3.6, 4.1, 5.2), derived mechanistically from the
  mixed-pixel argument (Section~1) rather than fitted post hoc to whichever
  shape metric happened to correlate (Discussion, Section~5.2, final
  paragraph).
\end{resp}

\subsection*{Specific comments}

\begin{resp}
  \item[Abstract, ``NDWI masks showed strong agreement'': N should be
  mentioned.] Every accuracy figure now states its N explicitly; the Abstract
  states N=62, and Section~4.2 documents the N=62$\to$59 accounting for the
  four-detector subset.
  \item[Abstract, computational cost of the proposed framework.] Added: a
  dedicated computational-cost audit (Section~3.8, Section~4.4, Figure
  \texttt{eecu\_svm\_vs\_vv.png}) measuring Earth Engine Compute Unit usage
  across detector configurations.
  \item[Introduction, ``relatively few studies... reservoir environments'' ---
  novelty of struggling conditions.] Rewritten: the Introduction (Section~1)
  now poses the open question directly (final paragraph before the
  objectives) rather than asserting that SAR struggles in these conditions as
  itself the finding.
  \item[Introduction, incidence-angle filtering not quantified unlike
  shoreline complexity.] Section~3.2 now states and justifies the
  orbit-selection rule explicitly (highest mean incidence angle, among
  orbits with $\geq$20 scenes and $\geq$90\% AOI coverage) as a controlled
  preprocessing choice held constant across both detectors. Shoreline
  complexity (A/P) remains the sole a-priori predictor under test, consistent
  with the reviewer's own assessment that it is the stronger contribution.
  \item[Introduction, medium/small reservoirs known to be difficult.] The
  global set spans $\sim$2--75~km$^2$ (Section~2.1) including medium and
  small reservoirs, and Section~4.1 shows why: small reservoirs tend toward
  low A/P, so A/P (not size per se) is the operative geometric control,
  explaining rather than merely acknowledging the difficulty.
  \item[Study Area, units notation (``$1.12\times10^9$~m$^3$'').] No longer
  applicable: the manuscript no longer reports storage-volume figures (see
  Summary of the reframing, above); all quantities are surface area
  (km$^2$/ha) or shoreline length (m).
  \item[Data Collection, ``not affected by data lacks due to cloudiness'' ---
  how is cloudiness an issue for SAR?] Reworded in the Introduction
  (Section~1) to state plainly that SAR is \emph{not} affected by cloud
  cover, which is its advantage over optical alternatives, not a claim that
  cloudiness affects SAR.
  \item[Figure 2, text overflow; incidence-angle ``why'' missing.] The
  workflow figure has been regenerated (\texttt{workflow\_diagram.pdf}), and
  Section~3.2 now states the quantitative rationale for the orbit-selection
  rule.
  \item[Classification, ``the labeled dataset'' undefined.] Fully specified
  in Section~3.3: water points where JRC occurrence $\geq$95\% (relaxed to
  $\geq$80\% where needed), land points from a 500--2000~m ring restricted to
  non-water WorldCover pixels with zero JRC occurrence, $\leq$500 points per
  class, fixed random seed. Figure \texttt{sample\_selection\_pozzillo.png}
  illustrates the construction concretely.
  \item[Classifier Selection, ``overall accuracy (96.12\%)'' --- train, test,
  or all?] No longer applicable: the manuscript no longer reports a single
  pixel-level confusion-matrix accuracy. Skill is reported exclusively as KGE
  (with its $r$, $\alpha$, $\beta$ components) against independent reference
  data (JRC or PlanetScope) on validation months (Section~3.7), never on the
  classifier's own training pixels.
  \item[Area Calculation, reservoir boundary / buffered region.] Section~3.5
  describes the post-processing chain (morphological gap-filling,
  vectorisation, centroid-inside retention with a largest-polygon fallback).
  The boundary itself is the JRC maximum-extent polygon (Section~3.3), which
  by construction already reflects the reservoir's historical maximum
  inundation, so it is not a nominal design boundary that could clip real
  flooding.
  \item[Temporal Filtering, LOWESS does not address wind artifacts; mismatch
  with Introduction.] Corrected: Section~3.5 now states plainly that LOWESS
  smoothing is applied for temporal consistency only, and that accuracy
  metrics are computed on the raw, unsmoothed classification. Wind's actual
  effect is instead tested directly in Sections~3.9/4.5, where we report an
  honest null result for a systematic wind effect (Discussion,
  Section~5.3), replacing the earlier, untested claim.
  \item[Volume Calculation, 2D polynomial vs.\ using AEV curves directly.] No
  longer applicable: the manuscript does not attempt area-to-volume
  conversion (see Summary of the reframing). Discussion Section~5.4
  identifies AEV reconstruction as future work, precisely because, as the
  reviewer rightly noted, that conversion carries separate uncertainties this
  paper is not positioned to resolve.
  \item[Validation, present only the validation/test period.] No longer
  applicable in its original form (there is no train/test split of a pixel
  classifier). The corresponding safeguard is that KGE (Section~3.7) is
  computed strictly over months where an independent external reference
  exists, never over training data.
  \item[Results, manual delineation undercuts the scalability claim.] Fixed
  directly: Section~3.3 describes a fully automated sample-selection
  procedure; no step in the pipeline involves manual delineation.
  \item[Figure 6, confusion-matrix broken reference and N mismatch.] No
  longer applicable: confusion-matrix figures have been removed. The
  four-detector comparison (Figure \texttt{kge\_4way.png}, Section~4.2)
  reports paired per-reservoir KGE with an internally consistent, explicitly
  documented N at every step.
  \item[Results, NDWI threshold sentence awkward; justification belongs in
  main text.] Section~3.7.2 now states the PlanetScope NDWI method concisely
  in the main text (scene-specific threshold, independent of the SAR
  workflow); full worked detail remains in the Supplementary Material.
  \item[Results, topographic shadowing does not fully explain
  overestimation.] No longer applicable in its original form: with the
  storage-volume analysis removed, this discrepancy no longer appears.
  Section~4.3 reports the PlanetScope comparison's bias ($\beta$) component
  directly, without attributing it solely to topography.
  \item[Figure 12, why not derive area from in-situ storage/AEV instead of
  PlanetScope?] Explained in Sections~2.2/3.7.2: PlanetScope is used
  specifically because it is independent of both the AEV relationship and
  the SAR pipeline, avoiding circularity; in-situ storage records would
  validate a volume step this paper no longer includes.
  \item[Table 3, ambiguous column headers (SVM vs.\ threshold).] No longer
  applicable in the same form: result tables were rebuilt with every
  detector (VV Otsu / SVM) named explicitly in each table and figure legend
  (Table~1; Section~4).
  \item[Discussion, physical mechanism behind high-storage underestimation
  not explained.] No longer applicable: no storage/volume analysis remains.
  The analogous geometric mechanism for area-classification error --- the
  mixed-pixel argument --- is instead stated mechanistically up front
  (Section~1; Section~3.6), not inferred post hoc from a discrepancy.
  \item[Discussion, ``scalability'' via a single Brazil AOI example.] That
  single-example transferability claim has been removed. Scalability is now
  demonstrated, not asserted, by testing the automated pipeline on 62
  reservoirs across five continents and 33 countries (Section~2.1), with the
  explicit caveat (Section~5.4) that only static, a-priori A/P, not any
  dynamic switching rule, is shown to generalise.
  \item[Discussion, compare against Otsu (1979) rather than an ad hoc simple
  threshold.] Implemented directly: Otsu's (1979) threshold, recomputed per
  scene, is now the single-polarisation baseline and headline comparison
  partner to the SVM throughout the manuscript (Sections~3.4.1, 4.2, 5.1).
  \item[Discussion, VH reduces wind-induced error --- evidence?] Corrected:
  Sections~3.9/4.5 test this directly using ERA5 wind speed at each
  overpass. We find no systematic wind effect on the between-detector
  divergence and report this null result honestly (Section~5.3), rather than
  asserting VH's benefit without evidence.
\end{resp}

\section*{Response to Reviewer 2}

\begin{resp}
  \item[A. Novelty.] As above: the manuscript's claim is no longer that GEE,
  VV/VH polarisation, or incidence-angle prioritisation are themselves novel.
  The contribution is the A/P mechanism (Sections~1, 3.6, 4.1, 5.2).
  \item[B.1 Introduction under-referenced / citations not well matched to
  claims.] The Introduction (Section~1) has been substantially rewritten and
  re-referenced throughout, with every claim tied to a specific citation
  (IPCC2023; Pokhrel2021; Cammalleri2022; Bevacqua2023; ISPRA2023;
  Biancamaria2016; Schwatke2015; Zhang2026; Lehner2011; Hao2024;
  Schwatke2020; Torres2012; Mullissa2021; Pekel2016; Gorelick2017;
  BhatpuriaEtAl2024; ValerioEtAl2024; PengEtAl2025; Bioresita2018;
  PenaLuque2021; Martinis2015; McQuillan2025).
  \item[B.2 Area vs.\ level claim overlooks morphology-dependence.] Reframed:
  Section~1 now presents water level and surface area as two complementary
  observables with different revisit and scaling trade-offs, rather than
  asserting area is generally superior. The morphology-dependence the
  reviewer raised is, in fact, the paper's central subject: A/P is precisely
  what governs how well area itself can be measured (Sections~3.6, 4.1).
  \item[B.3 Outdated AEV argument undermines rather than supports the
  rationale.] Reworded (Section~1): AEV relationships needing updates is now
  presented as an open problem this paper does not solve --- motivating
  Schwatke-style future work (Discussion, Section~5.4) --- not as a
  justification for preferring area over level.
  \item[C. Area of Study needs a summary table of reservoir attributes.]
  Added: Table~1 (Section~2.1) lists per-reservoir country,
  climate zone, surface area, and A/P for all 62 global reservoirs plus the
  four Sicilian near-truth reservoirs.
  \item[D.1 Sections 2.2--2.5 disorganised relative to Figure 2's
  workflow.] Section~3 has been fully reorganised around the five workflow
  stages shown in Figure~2 (the workflow diagram): 3.1 overview, 3.2
  acquisition/preprocessing, 3.3 automated training, 3.4 the two detectors,
  3.5 post-processing/area, 3.6 morphometry, 3.7 validation, 3.8 cost audit,
  3.9 radiometric/climate limits --- a direct one-to-one match to the
  workflow figure.
  \item[D.2 IW3 / ``when necessary'' needs clarification.] Section~3.2 now
  states the orbit-selection rule explicitly and its rationale (see Reviewer
  1, specific comment on incidence-angle filtering, above).
  \item[D.3 Multiple classifiers (SVM, RF, CART, GTB, Minimum Distance)
  under-described.] The comparison has been narrowed to exactly two,
  thoroughly described detectors --- Otsu (Section~3.4.1) and SVM
  (Section~3.4.2, with kernel, cost, and $\gamma$ stated explicitly) ---
  removing the under-justified multi-algorithm comparison entirely.
  \item[D.4 Equation (1), the $10^4$ factor unexplained.] No longer
  applicable: area is now stated directly as the pixel-area sum of the
  retained water mask (Section~3.5), without the equation the reviewer
  flagged.
  \item[D.5 Justify the representativeness of the four validation
  reservoirs.] Section~2.2: the four Sicilian reservoirs are now explicitly
  an additional near-truth PlanetScope tier layered on the 62-reservoir
  global JRC tier (not the sole validation), chosen to span contrasting
  morphometries and A/P values within Sicily (Figure
  \texttt{sicily\_4lakes\_3d.png}) rather than to represent the full global
  study area by themselves.
  \item[E.1 Data-collection subsection misplaced in Results.] Fixed: all
  data and methods content now resides in Section~3 only; Section~4
  (Results) contains no data-collection description.
  \item[E.2 Results section reorganisation.] Section~4 is restructured into
  five subsections matching the paper's actual claims (4.1 A/P a priori, 4.2
  four-way detector comparison, 4.3 PlanetScope validation, 4.4 cost, 4.5
  wind/climate), no longer interleaved with workflow-step descriptions.
  \item[E.3 Hyperparameter tuning (three values of C and $\gamma$)
  unclear.] No longer applicable: the SVM now uses a single, fixed, fully
  stated configuration ($C=1$, $\gamma=0.01$; Section~3.4.2) rather than an
  ambiguously reported grid search.
  \item[E.4 Storage discrepancy interpretation inconsistent with
  sedimentation direction.] No longer applicable: the storage-volume
  analysis has been removed in its entirety (see Summary of the reframing),
  so the disputed claim no longer appears.
  \item[E.5 Only four of 41 reservoirs shown; extend further.] Addressed by
  extending well beyond the original 41-reservoir Sicilian study area to 62
  reservoirs on five continents as the primary comparison (Section~2.1), with
  the four Sicilian reservoirs retained only as an additional near-truth
  layer (Section~2.2).
  \item[F. Robustness/generalisability across all 41 reservoirs unclear.]
  Representativeness is now argued at the 62-reservoir, five-continent,
  A/P-stratified level (Section~2.1, Figure \texttt{study\_area\_map}), not
  inferred from four of 41 Sicilian reservoirs alone.
\end{resp}
