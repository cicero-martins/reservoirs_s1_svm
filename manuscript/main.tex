\begin{abstract}
% NOTE: the four-way global comparison is currently N=28 (10 complementary
% reservoirs pending). Stable numbers: A/P rho=0.57; between-method rho=-0.41;
% EECU fixed-dual 0.69x, per-scene dual 1.21x VV-Otsu. See project memory.
The area-to-perimeter ratio (A/P) of a reservoir shoreline, computable in
advance from global databases, predicts both the accuracy of Sentinel-1
surface-area monitoring and the water classifier a reservoir requires. We test
this in a single cloud-based Google Earth Engine pipeline that compares two
per-scene-adaptive detectors: a single-polarisation VV Otsu threshold and a
dual-polarisation (VV+VH) support vector machine (SVM) re-trained for each
acquisition, with training samples drawn automatically from the JRC Global
Surface Water dataset. The comparison covers 38 reservoirs on five continents,
referenced to JRC surface water, and four Sicilian reservoirs validated against
3~m PlanetScope near-truth. A/P predicts surface-area skill (Spearman
$\rho = 0.57$), setting a geometric ceiling that is high for compact reservoirs
and low and variable for narrow, dendritic ones. Per-scene adaptivity, not the
number of polarisations, drives accuracy: a fixed-training dual SVM is the least
accurate, whereas the two per-scene detectors tie on the median, the
dual-polarisation advantage appearing only at low A/P ($\rho = -0.41$). Cost
follows the same axis, so the accurate per-scene dual SVM is the most expensive
method, about $1.2\times$ the VV threshold. A/P therefore selects the cheapest
sufficient classifier, reserving the dual SVM for complex shorelines. We release
the workflow as an interactive Google Earth Engine application that auto-trains
from JRC and applies this A/P-based selection over more than 35,000 global
reservoirs (Global Dam Watch). It provides a reliable, low-cost, and transferable
basis for the surface-area input to reservoir storage monitoring in data-scarce
regions.
\end{abstract}
