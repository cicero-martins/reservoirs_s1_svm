\begin{abstract}
This study presents an operational workflow for monitoring water surface
area and storage dynamics using Sentinel-1 SAR imagery and machine
learning on Google Earth Engine (GEE), demonstrated across 41 reservoirs
in Sicily~($n=41$). The preprocessing pipeline includes incidence-angle
filtering that prioritizes IW3 acquisitions ($\sim$42--46$^{\circ}$),
reducing angular backscatter variability and improving temporal
consistency. Water detection is performed using a Support Vector Machine
(SVM) classifier trained on VV and VH polarizations, which outperformed
threshold-based methods and other classifiers available under GEE.
Classified masks were refined through morphological filtering and converted
into surface-area and volume time series using reservoir-specific
area--volume curves. Validation against high-resolution PlanetScope NDWI
masks showed strong agreement ($R^{2}>0.95$; KGE up to 0.96;
$n=\text{X}$ image pairs). To interpret performance differences, a
shoreline morphometric analysis based on the area-to-perimeter ratio (A/P)
was conducted, revealing that reservoirs with smoother, more compact
shorelines exhibit higher SAR classification accuracy, whereas highly
irregular systems show larger uncertainties. Comparison with official
storage records confirmed very good performance for large reservoirs and
highlighted limitations related to shoreline complexity and outdated
bathymetric relationships in smaller systems. The workflow provides a
cloud-based approach for satellite-driven reservoir storage monitoring in
data-scarce regions.
\end{abstract}
