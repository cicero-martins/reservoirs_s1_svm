\section{Study Area and Reservoir Sample}
\label{sec:study_area}

The experimental design rests on two complementary reservoir samples, chosen
to separate the two questions this paper addresses. The first,
whether the area-to-perimeter ratio (A/P) predicts SAR classification skill
and governs the choice between a single- and a dual-polarisation detector,
requires \emph{breadth}: many reservoirs spanning a wide range of shoreline
geometry and climate. The second, how the resulting surface-area series
compare against near-truth reference data, requires \emph{depth}: a few
reservoirs with independent, high-resolution validation. We therefore combine
a global pilot of 38 reservoirs referenced against the JRC Global Surface
Water dataset \citep{Pekel2016} with four Sicilian reservoirs validated
against high-resolution PlanetScope imagery.

\subsection{Global pilot: 38 reservoirs across five continents}
\label{sec:study_global}

The global pilot comprises 38 reservoirs distributed across fourteen
countries and five continents (Europe, Africa, Asia, the Americas, and
Oceania), spanning a broad range of K\"oppen--Geiger climate types from
tropical to humid-continental (Figure~\ref{fig:map_global}; per-reservoir
country, climate, area, and A/P in Table~\ref{tab:pilot}). Surface areas
range from roughly 2.4 to 50~\si{\kilo\metre\squared} (about 240--5000~ha).
The reservoirs sit in contrasting biomes and, with them, contrasting wind
regimes (the radiometric factor examined in
Section~\ref{sec:methods_wind}), so SAR performance is probed well beyond a
single climatic setting.

Across the sample, static shoreline compactness (the A/P of the JRC
maximum-water-extent polygon, computable a priori)
ranges from 66 to 461~m. At the low-A/P end are narrow, dendritic gorge
reservoirs impounded in steep valleys (e.g.\ Sau and Susqueda on the Ter,
Rappbode, Grandval), whose long, convoluted shorelines maximise the
mixed-pixel fraction; at the high-A/P end are compact, bowl- or plain-shaped
impoundments with short, smooth shorelines (e.g.\ Harlan County, Umbuluzi,
Erfenis, Hubbard Creek). Figure~\ref{fig:map_global} colours each reservoir
by A/P, showing that this geometric gradient is sampled across all regions
rather than confined to one. Candidate reservoirs and their area--storage
attributes were drawn from the Global Reservoir and Dam databases
\citep[GRanD, GRDL;][]{Lehner2011, Hao2024}, and their water-body polygons
from the JRC layer \citep{Pekel2016}.

\subsection{Sicilian near-truth reservoirs}
\label{sec:study_sicily}

Four Sicilian reservoirs (Ancipa, Pozzillo, Poma, and Rosamarina)
provide an independent, high-resolution validation anchor that breaks the
inter-product circularity of comparing one 30~m product (JRC, derived from
optical Landsat) against a 10~m SAR product. Sicily, the largest island in
the Mediterranean ($\sim$25{,}441~\si{\kilo\metre\squared}), has a
predominantly Mediterranean climate (Csa in the K\"oppen--Geiger system) with
hot, dry summers and mild, wet winters, and a marked orographic gradient
between semi-arid coastal plains and the wetter Madonie and Nebrodi mountain
ranges \citep{Costantini2013}. Natural lakes are rare, so artificial
reservoirs form the backbone of the island's water supply; over the past
decade they have repeatedly reached critically low levels during the dry
season \citep{ISPRA2023}, making them a demanding and policy-relevant test
bed.

The four reference reservoirs span contrasting morphometries: Pozzillo, the
largest, is a broad, relatively shallow basin; Poma and Rosamarina are
intermediate; and Ancipa is a small, steep-sided, valley-confined reservoir
in the Nebrodi range whose narrow, predominantly east--west-oriented body
represents the low-A/P, hardest-to-monitor end of the range. For each, cloud-
free PlanetScope SuperDove imagery ($\sim$3~m) acquired between May~2024 and
May~2025 yields near-truth NDWI water masks used to validate the
Sentinel-1--derived surface areas (Section~\ref{sec:methods}).
