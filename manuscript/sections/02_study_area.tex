\section{Study Area and Reservoir Sample}
\label{sec:study_area}

The experimental design rests on two complementary reservoir samples, each
serving a different purpose. The global set answers to the need for
\emph{breadth}: many reservoirs spanning a wide range of shoreline geometry
and climate, needed to test whether the area-to-perimeter ratio (A/P)
predicts SAR classification skill and whether the added complexity of a
dual-polarisation detector is justified. The Sicilian set answers to the need
for \emph{depth}: a few reservoirs with independent, high-resolution
validation, needed to check the resulting surface-area series against
near-truth reference data. We therefore combine a global set of
62 reservoirs referenced against the JRC Global Surface Water dataset
\citep{Pekel2016} with four Sicilian reservoirs validated against
high-resolution PlanetScope imagery.

\subsection{Global set: 62 reservoirs across five continents}
\label{sec:study_global}

The global set comprises 62 reservoirs distributed across 33 countries and
five continents (Europe, Africa, Asia, the Americas, and Oceania), spanning
a broad range of K\"oppen--Geiger climate types from equatorial tropical to
humid-continental (Figure~\ref{fig:map_global}; per-reservoir country,
climate, area, and A/P in Table~\ref{tab:pilot}). Surface areas span roughly
2 to 75~\si{\kilo\metre\squared}. The reservoirs sit in contrasting biomes and
climates, so SAR performance is probed well beyond a single climatic setting.
Climate's contribution to accuracy, beyond A/P, is examined in
Section~\ref{sec:res_climate}. This is the set retained after the
reference-quality screen described in Section~\ref{sec:val_global}. It excludes
14 reservoirs whose JRC optical reference proved unreliable, so that reported
accuracy reflects the SAR classifiers rather than artefacts of the comparison
data.

Across the set, static shoreline compactness (the A/P of the JRC
maximum-water-extent polygon, computable a priori)
ranges from 62 to 461~m. At the low-A/P end are narrow, dendritic gorge
reservoirs impounded in steep valleys (e.g.\ Sau and Susqueda on the Ter,
Rappbode, Grandval), whose long, convoluted shorelines maximise the
mixed-pixel fraction. At the high-A/P end are compact, bowl- or plain-shaped
impoundments with short, smooth shorelines (e.g.\ Harlan County, Umbuluzi,
Erfenis, Hubbard Creek). Figure~\ref{fig:map_global} colours each reservoir
by A/P, showing that this geometric gradient is sampled across all regions
rather than confined to one. Candidate reservoirs and their area attributes
were drawn from the Global Reservoir and Dam databases
\citep[GRanD, GRDL;][]{Lehner2011, Hao2024}, and their water-body polygons
from the JRC maximum-extent layer \citep{Pekel2016}.

\subsection{Sicilian near-truth reservoirs}
\label{sec:study_sicily}

Four Sicilian reservoirs (Ancipa, Pozzillo, Poma, and Rosamarina) provide an
independent, high-resolution validation anchor. The global tier references
Sentinel-1-derived areas against JRC, itself a 30~m Landsat-derived product:
at low A/P, JRC's own mixed-pixel fraction grows by the same geometric
mechanism this paper tests for SAR, so a reservoir that appears poorly
monitored there could reflect the coarseness of the reference as much as any
weakness of the SAR classifier. The 3~m PlanetScope imagery used here shrinks
that mixed-pixel fraction by roughly an order of magnitude, giving a
near-truth check that is not itself limited by the same resolution-driven
mechanism. Sicily, the largest island in
the Mediterranean ($\sim$25{,}441~\si{\kilo\metre\squared}), has a
predominantly Mediterranean climate (Csa in the K\"oppen--Geiger system) with
hot, dry summers and mild, wet winters, and a marked orographic gradient
between semi-arid coastal plains and the wetter Madonie and Nebrodi mountain
ranges \citep{Costantini2013}. Natural lakes are rare, so artificial
reservoirs form the backbone of the island's water supply. Over the past
decade they have repeatedly reached critically low levels during the dry
season \citep{ISPRA2023}, making them a demanding and policy-relevant test
bed.

The four reference reservoirs span contrasting morphometries
(Figure~\ref{fig:sicily_3d}). Pozzillo, the
largest, is a broad, relatively shallow basin. Poma and Rosamarina are
intermediate, and Ancipa is a small, steep-sided, valley-confined reservoir
in the Nebrodi range whose narrow, predominantly east--west-oriented body
represents the low-A/P, hardest-to-monitor end of the range. For each, cloud-
free PlanetScope SuperDove imagery ($\sim$3~m) acquired between May~2024 and
May~2025 yields near-truth NDWI water masks used to validate the
Sentinel-1--derived surface areas (Section~\ref{sec:methods}).
