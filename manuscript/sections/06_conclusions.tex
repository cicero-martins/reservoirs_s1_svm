\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

Using a fully automated Google Earth Engine workflow applied to 62
reference-quality-screened reservoirs on five continents, together with four
Sicilian reservoirs validated against 3~m PlanetScope imagery, we tested the
area-to-perimeter ratio as an a-priori predictor of SAR surface-area accuracy
and asked whether the added complexity of a dual-polarisation,
machine-learning classifier is justified. Three conclusions follow. First,
A/P is a strong, geographically general predictor of the geometric ceiling on
monitorability (Spearman $\rho = 0.51$) and can be computed from global
databases before any image is processed, which makes it a practical tool for
screening where SAR monitoring will succeed. Second, the added complexity is
not justified: a per-scene single-polarisation Otsu threshold is as accurate
as a per-scene dual-polarisation SVM across the global set, is the better
detector in most reservoirs, and is also the cheapest to run, while a
fixed-training SVM is the least accurate, so per-scene adaptivity matters more
than the number of polarisations. Third, climate is a secondary but genuine
modulator of the geometric ceiling: humid, vegetated reservoirs are tracked
less well at given A/P even after every reservoir with an unreliable optical
reference is excluded, marking where an all-weather SAR record is most
valuable and where climate, rather than reference quality, is now the more
defensible explanation.

Future work should extend the framework from surface area to storage volume,
supported by updated area--elevation--volume relationships, and should address
the radiometric residual directly, for example through a dynamic threshold on
the cross-polarised or ratio channel for the extreme low-water, low-A/P regime
where a second polarisation can still help. Combined with the automated,
transferable training and the simple per-scene detector adopted here, released
as an interactive application over more than 35,000 global reservoirs, these
steps would move the workflow towards operational reservoir monitoring in
data-scarce regions.
