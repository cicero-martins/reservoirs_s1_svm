\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Reservoirs regulate water supply, irrigation, hydropower and flood risk, and their
operation depends on knowing how much water is stored as a function of level, i.e.\ the
area--elevation--volume (AEV) relationship. That relationship is not static. Sediment
delivered by the contributing basin accumulates on the reservoir floor, progressively
reducing capacity, yet for a large fraction of the world's dams the AEV curve in
operational use still dates to construction, decades ago, and no longer reflects the
present geometry of the basin \citep{BrunoEtAl2003}. The consequence is a systematic,
and usually unquantified, over-estimation of available storage. Correcting it by
repeating the bathymetric surveys that produced the original curves is expensive and
logistically demanding, so re-surveys are rare and most reservoirs continue to be
managed with a stage record and an obsolete curve --- if they are monitored at all.

Satellite remote sensing offers a way to update this information without field
campaigns, because two complementary and freely available signals together contain
everything the AEV relationship requires. Satellite altimetry measures water-surface
\emph{elevation}: nadir radar missions, harmonised in databases such as DAHITI
\citep{Schwatke2015}, and, since 2022, the wide-swath mission SWOT
\citep{Biancamaria2016}. Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR), and in particular the
free, twice-weekly Sentinel-1 archive \citep{Torres2012}, measures water-surface
\emph{area} directly, day or night and through cloud, with a reliability that a
companion study shows can be predicted \emph{a priori} from shoreline geometry
\citep{PaperOne}. \citet{Schwatke2020} combined water masks with altimetry to
reconstruct the hypsometry of lakes. Here we adapt that principle to the reservoir
problem and, decisively, to the large population of reservoirs that have \emph{neither}
an in-situ gauge \emph{nor} an altimetry ground track passing over them.

The idea we pursue is that the shoreline exposed as a reservoir draws down is a sequence
of contour lines at known water levels: stacking Sentinel-1 waterlines ordered by level
reconstructs a digital elevation model (DEM) of the emerged basin, from which an updated
AEV curve and a storage time series follow by integration. The water level needed to
label each waterline is the single indispensable ingredient. Where a gauge exists this
is trivial; where it does not, the level must itself be remotely sensed. We therefore
treat the water-level source as a variable to be substituted: keeping the SAR area and
the reconstruction fixed, we replace the in-situ gauge first with nadir altimetry and
then with SWOT, whose swath geometry observes reservoirs regardless of track and thus
addresses precisely the ungauged, track-missed case that has excluded most reservoirs
from altimetry-based monitoring.

The contributions of this paper are: (i) a fully remote-sensing pipeline that
reconstructs updated reservoir bathymetry and storage from Sentinel-1 waterlines and a
remote water level; (ii) a validation on nine Sicilian reservoirs against a
December-2025 echo-sounder survey and recent official bathymetric curves; (iii) a
water-level-substitution experiment showing that SWOT altimetry yields storage
equivalent to an in-situ gauge and to field truth; (iv) an independent, cross-sensor
corroboration using PlanetScope optical waterlines; and (v) an uncertainty and
observability analysis, together with an open interactive tool, that links
reconstruction reliability to shoreline geometry and drawdown amplitude and makes the
approach usable in practice.
