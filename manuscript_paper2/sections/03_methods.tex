\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Bathymetric reconstruction: DEM and scalar hypsometry}
\label{sec:dem}
Let $\{M_i\}$ be the Sentinel-1 binary water masks and $\{h_i\}$ their water levels,
sorted ascending by $h_i$. Pixels wet in the smallest mask (the always-wet core) are
assigned the lowest level $h_0$. A pixel that first becomes wet between consecutive
levels is a shoreline exposed in that increment and is assigned the mid-level
$(h_{i-1}+h_i)/2$. The resulting elevation field is closed over the union footprint,
interior gaps are filled by linear interpolation (\texttt{scipy.griddata}), and the
DEM is smoothed with a Gaussian filter ($\sigma=2$ px, $=20$~m). This adapts
\citet{Schwatke2020} to Sentinel-1 waterlines. The procedure is agnostic to the mask
source: any sequence of binary water masks with associated water levels stacks
identically, which we exploit to cross-validate the reconstruction with an independent
optical sensor (Section~\ref{sec:xsensor}). The DEM reconstructs only the
drawdown-\emph{exposed} band $[\,\text{floor},\text{max}\,]$; the permanently wet
deep zone is not observed.

As an alternative that needs only the area series, each SAR area is instead paired
with the nearest water level (within $\pm10$ days) and a power-law hypsometry is
fitted,
\begin{equation}
  A(h) = a\,(h-h_0)^{b},
\label{eq:powerlaw}
\end{equation}
which is integrated to storage $V(h)=\int A\,\mathrm{d}h$. The DEM gives the spatial
bathymetry and change maps; the scalar fit gives a robust storage curve where the
observed level range is wide. Both see only the exposed band.

\subsection{Independent validation of the water-level and mask inputs}
\label{sec:xsensor}
Two further experiments test whether the reconstruction is robust to substituting
each of its two inputs in turn: the water level, and the water mask.

Keeping the SAR area and the reconstruction fixed, we swap the water-level source
between two independent tiers, the in-situ \emph{gauge} and \emph{SWOT} altimetry,
refit Eq.~\eqref{eq:powerlaw} for each, and integrate over the common observed band
to compare the resulting storage to each other and to the field/survey truth.

Because the reconstruction (Section~\ref{sec:dem}) is agnostic to the mask source, we
independently rebuild the bathymetry from PlanetScope 3~m optical water masks and
compare it, pixel-wise and in AEV, to the Sentinel-1 reconstruction over the four
reservoirs PlanetScope covers (2024--2025). Water is delineated per scene by a
normalised difference water index,
$\mathrm{NDWI}=(\rho_\text{green}-\rho_\text{NIR})/(\rho_\text{green}+\rho_\text{NIR})$,
thresholded per scene. As with the water-level ladder above, each site is labelled
from one of two deliberately independent tiers --- the in-situ gauge by default, or
SWOT where the gauge is degenerate over the optical window (chosen automatically from
the gauge's interquartile range over the mask dates, $<0.5$~m indicating a stuck
sensor) --- to avoid mixing datums within a single reconstruction. This provides an
independent, higher-resolution optical check and, for \emph{Ancipa} and
\emph{Pozzillo} (which lack a modern bathymetric survey), an independent near-truth.
Because optical detection carries error modes orthogonal to SAR (surface algae and
macrophytes, turbidity, sun glint), agreement between the two reconstructions is
strong evidence that neither is dominated by sensor-specific error.

\subsection{Capacity-change metrics and uncertainty}
\label{sec:uncertainty}
Capacity change relative to the design curve is quantified two ways: \emph{band}
(relative to the DEM floor, i.e.\ the SAR-observable exposed zone, the fair
comparison against survey truth) and \emph{total} (the full design curve, including
the deep zone below the floor that SAR cannot see). Because sedimentation concentrates
in the deep zone, the SAR band change is a validated \emph{lower bound} on total loss.
We additionally quantify how much of each reservoir's total capacity the exposed band
represents, using the design curve's own volume at the DEM floor as the deep-zone
estimate: every core reservoir's design curve is tabulated down to at or near zero
volume, i.e.\ its own lowest surveyed point is effectively the pre-impoundment valley
floor, so this estimate is read directly from data already in use rather than requiring
a new terrain source.

The one field-validated reservoir (Garcia) yields a normalised, elevation-dependent
bias model $\mathrm{bias}(f)$, $f=(z-\text{floor})/(\text{max}-\text{floor})$, applied
to the other reservoirs. Two distinct uncertainties follow from it. The first is
calibration uncertainty in the bias model itself: we draw 500 samples of its two
regression coefficients from their joint covariance and propagate each through the
band-capacity-change calculation, giving a 5th--95th-percentile confidence interval
that is narrow ($\pm0.6\%$) because it reflects only the fitted line's own
uncertainty, not independent per-pixel noise. The second, larger, uncertainty is
whether the bias model \emph{transfers} to other reservoirs at all: the RMS of the
bias-corrected error against independent truth at Rosamarina and Poma (excluding the
self-calibrated Garcia) is ${\approx}\pm10$ percentage points, the dominant term in
the overall budget.

\subsection{Implementation and code release}
\label{sec:tool}
The full pipeline --- Earth Engine mask export, water-level ingestion, DEM
reconstruction, and AEV/storage integration --- is released as open-source Python
code, together with a Streamlit application that displays, for a selected reservoir,
the reconstructed 2D/3D bathymetry, the AEV curves against design and survey
references, and the multi-period change map, and offers the DEM for download. The
application currently runs locally from the released code rather than as a hosted
service.
