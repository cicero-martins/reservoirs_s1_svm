\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Validation of the SAR area and water-level inputs}
\label{sec:res_inputvalid}
The Sentinel-1 water-surface-area input is not re-validated here: its reliability, and its
dependence on shoreline area-to-perimeter ratio (A/P), is established in \citet{PaperOne}
and simply applied (Section~\ref{sec:inputvalid}).

\label{sec:res_ladder}
The water level is the other required input, and we test its substitution directly: keeping
the SAR area fixed, the in-situ gauge and SWOT altimetry are paired separately with the same
area series, fitted to the same power-law hypsometry, and the resulting band storages compared
to each other and to field/survey truth. Across the four reservoirs with an independent
truth (Garcia, Poma, Rosamarina, Arancio), SWOT storage differs from truth by a mean of
$2.2$~\si{Mm^3} (Garcia $-3.5$, Poma $-0.7$, Rosamarina $-4.1$, Arancio $-0.7$~\si{Mm^3}), with
SWOT--gauge water-level RMSE of $0.8$--$3.7$~m. For the two reservoirs without a survey, gauge
and SWOT storage agree to $-0.3$~\si{Mm^3} (Pozzillo) but diverge at low A/P (Ancipa,
$-3.2$~\si{Mm^3}, water-level RMSE $12$~m) --- the same low-A/P degradation seen throughout.
We further find that DAHITI is not an independent source for these reservoirs: its records
begin exactly at the SWOT launch (2023-07) and match SWOT epochs at $>96\%$ of dates with a
water-level offset of only $+0.2$ to $-0.6$~m, i.e.\ DAHITI is a reprocessed SWOT product. The
gauge-free result therefore rests on gauge-versus-SWOT, not on a third independent tier.

Three of the nine gauges used elsewhere in this study have a documented malfunction window,
each resolved with the same substitution logic rather than by discarding the affected dates
(Section~\ref{sec:data}): Rosamarina's sensor froze at two different flat readings across
$\sim$19 months combined; Garcia's sensor reads the dry lakebed floor instead of the true level
whenever the reservoir empties below $\sim$176~m; and Nicoletti's sensor was inoperative until
late 2023. In each case SWOT (Rosamarina, Garcia) or the design curve inverted from official
monthly volumes (Nicoletti) fills the gap. That three of nine reservoirs needed this
substitution in practice, not merely in principle, is itself evidence for pairing SAR with a
gauge-independent altimetry source rather than relying on in-situ networks alone.

\subsection{Reconstructed bathymetry and capacity change}
\label{sec:res_change}
With both inputs validated, the waterline-stacked DEMs reconstruct the exposed basin of all
nine reservoirs over Periods A (2014--2016) and B (2022--2026), yielding 2D/3D bathymetry and
A-vs-B change maps (Table~\ref{tab:capacity}, Fig.~\ref{fig:change}). Relative to the design
curve, the SAR-observable (band) capacity change ranges from a negligible $-0.3\%$ (Pozzillo)
to $-47.3\%$ (Olivo). The exposed band itself represents a widely varying share of each
reservoir's total design-curve volume, $39.1\%$--$83.8\%$ (mean $69\%$ across the eight
reservoirs for which this ratio is well posed) --- so for most reservoirs the majority of
storage is directly observed, but the fraction is far from uniform and is smallest exactly
where A/P is lowest. Ancipa is excluded from this ratio: its design curve's lowest tabulated
quota sits above the corrected DEM's true floor, so the raw ratio exceeds 100\% (a
design-curve extrapolation artifact, not a real deep zone); we report it as $100\%$ with this
caveat rather than force a spurious number. Nicoletti's Period-A and Period-B water-level
ranges share only a narrow overlap ($\sim$12\% of change-map pixels), so its capacity-change
estimate rests on a correspondingly narrow elevation window and should be read with that
caveat.

The apparent band loss itself tracks A/P: the three lowest-A/P reservoirs (Olivo $50.7$~m,
Ancipa $90.5$~m, Nicoletti $119.7$~m) show the largest losses ($-47.3\%$, $-41.0\%$,
$-40.2\%$), while the two highest-A/P reservoirs (Poma $190.1$~m, Pozzillo $240.5$~m) show the
smallest ($-14.7\%$, $-0.3\%$). Section~\ref{sec:res_curves} shows this is only partly a real
sedimentation signal: at low A/P the same shoreline-geometry limitation that constrains SAR
area retrieval \citep{PaperOne} also degrades the DEM reconstruction, so part of the apparent
loss is reconstruction uncertainty rather than true capacity change --- a pattern quantified
directly in Section~\ref{sec:res_unc}.

\subsection{Validation against independent references}
\label{sec:res_valid}

\subsubsection{Field validation against an echo-sounder survey (Garcia)}
\label{sec:res_garcia}
Against the December-2025 echo-sounder survey, the Garcia DEM has an overall bias of
$+0.54$~m and RMSE $2.43$~m in the SAR-observable range ($\mathrm{R}^2=0.67$). A residual
elevation dependence remains, from $+1.3$~m near the floor ($175$--$176$~m) to $-2.1$~m near
the top ($189$--$190$~m) (Fig.~\ref{fig:garcia}). At full pool, the survey reveals an
$11.80$~\si{Mm^3} storage loss versus design ($25\%$ of design capacity), of which the SAR DEM
detects $13.36$~\si{Mm^3} ($28\%$) --- the SAR estimate \emph{slightly exceeds} rather than
under-detects the surveyed loss, capturing $113\%$ of it.

\subsubsection{Validation against recent bathymetric curves}
\label{sec:res_curves}
Six reservoirs have a recent official area--volume curve as a secondary reference
(Table~\ref{tab:capacity}, rightmost column). The comparison is informative less for the raw
size of any single gap than for its pattern across A/P. At high A/P, SAR and the official
curve agree closely: Poma's official curve shows essentially no net loss ($-0.5\%$ band,
$+2.9\%$ total), while the SAR DEM shows a modest $-14.7\%$ band deficit attributable to the
low-elevation reconstruction bias characterised in Section~\ref{sec:res_unc} rather than real
sedimentation --- a useful negative-control case. Rosamarina and Arancio show a similar pattern
at a smaller offset (SAR $-18.1\%$/$-17.3\%$ versus survey/curve $-7.3\%$/$-3.7\%$ band). At
low A/P the gap widens sharply: Castello ($-31.2\%$ vs.\ $-23.4\%$), Olivo ($-47.3\%$ vs.\
$-3.6\%$) and Nicoletti ($-40.2\%$ vs.\ $-6.2\%$) all show SAR over-estimating the true band
loss by a wide margin, Olivo and Nicoletti most severely. This is the same A/P-ordered pattern
already visible in Section~\ref{sec:res_change}: reconstruction reliability, not just apparent
capacity change, degrades as A/P falls.

\subsubsection{Cross-sensor validation: PlanetScope optical reconstruction}
\label{sec:res_xsensor}
Reconstructing the bathymetry from PlanetScope 3~m optical masks (Section~\ref{sec:xsensor};
5--39 masks per reservoir) and comparing to the Sentinel-1 DEM, the two independent sensors
agree closely across the observable band at medium/high A/P: the per-pixel elevation RMSE is
$1.32$~m (Pozzillo, A/P~240), $1.98$~m (Poma, A/P~190) and $3.26$~m (Rosamarina, A/P~187) ---
of the order of the Garcia \emph{field} RMSE ($2.43$~m) (Fig.~\ref{fig:planet}). The agreement
is strongest precisely where no modern field survey exists: the optical and SAR band storages
match to $4.0$~\si{Mm^3} at \emph{Pozzillo} ($22.6$ vs $26.7$) and to $0.9$~\si{Mm^3} at
\emph{Rosamarina} ($13.7$ vs $12.9$), giving these reservoirs an independent near-truth.
Rosamarina also illustrates why the water-level source matters: its in-situ gauge was stuck
for $\sim$19 months across the 2024--2026 window (Section~\ref{sec:res_ladder}); substituting
SWOT --- which, like the optical masks, records the real drawdown --- restores a physical
bathymetry and the reported cross-sensor agreement. The two reconstructions diverge (RMSE
$6.06$~m) only at \emph{Ancipa} (A/P~90), where the narrow, low-A/P geometry degrades both the
optical masks (only 5 usable, structurally limited by PlanetScope's coverage predating
Ancipa's gauge) and the SWOT water level --- the same A/P limit identified throughout. Because
optical NDWI and SAR backscatter fail under orthogonal conditions, agreement between them is
strong evidence the reconstruction is not dominated by sensor-specific error.

\subsection{Uncertainty}
\label{sec:res_unc}
The Garcia-derived normalised bias model (bias$(f) = +2.79 - 4.40f$~m, $f$ the fractional
height in the exposed band) transfers only partially to the other reservoirs: applying it
lowers the mean absolute error against independent truth from $9.2\%$ (raw) to $7.8\%$
(corrected), an improvement but not a resolution. The dominant, systematic transfer
uncertainty on the band capacity change is $\pm11.3$ percentage points (RMS of the
bias-corrected error at Rosamarina, Poma and Garcia's own independent references); the
random per-pixel calibration component is far smaller ($\pm0.8\%$ median) because it reflects
only the fitted bias line's own uncertainty, not independent per-pixel noise
(Fig.~\ref{fig:uncertainty}). This transfer uncertainty is consistent with, and does not
fully explain, the much larger low-A/P discrepancies in Section~\ref{sec:res_curves}
(Olivo, Nicoletti): those are better read as a genuine A/P-driven reconstruction-reliability
ceiling, the same mechanism established for SAR area retrieval itself \citep{PaperOne}, rather
than a correctable bias.
