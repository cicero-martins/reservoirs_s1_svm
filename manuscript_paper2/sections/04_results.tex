\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Validation of the SAR area and water-level inputs}
\label{sec:res_inputvalid}
The Sentinel-1 water-surface-area input carries the reliability \citet{PaperOne} establishes
across 66 reservoirs, including 5 Sicilian ones: surface-area skill correlates with shoreline
area-to-perimeter ratio (Spearman $\rho=0.51$), reaching a median KGE of $0.93$ at high A/P
($\geq200$~m) against $0.48$ at low A/P ($<100$~m), with $82\%$ of that global set clearing
KGE $\geq0.5$. The simpler per-scene VV threshold used here matches its dual-polarisation SVM
alternative at a lower computational cost in the same comparison, so we apply it directly
rather than re-validating it (Section~\ref{sec:inputvalid}).

\label{sec:res_ladder}
For the water level, Poma offers the clearest single case, showing the textbook signature of
a pure offset rather than a real water-level disagreement, with a correlation of $r=1.00$
before correction and the mean offset alone accounting for essentially all of the raw RMSE.
Correcting it changes RMSE, which is sensitive to an absolute offset, but leaves KGE (whose
mean-ratio term is small relative to these reservoirs' absolute elevations) essentially
unaffected. Fig.~\ref{fig:wlsource} plots the cleaned SWOT against the gauge over the full
SWOT era (2023-07 onward) for all nine reservoirs, and Table~\ref{tab:wlrmse} summarises the
agreement as both an RMSE and a KGE, the same skill score used throughout \citet{PaperOne}, so
the two studies' notions of ``agreement'' are directly comparable. We call a source
\emph{acceptable} when its error is small relative to the reconstruction's own elevation bias
(Section~\ref{sec:res_unc}, a few metres in amplitude), requiring KGE $\geq0.8$ once cleaned.
Seven of nine reservoirs clear this bar: Arancio, Poma, Garcia, Rosamarina, Olivo, Castello
and Ancipa, RMSE $0.14$--$0.63$~m, KGE $0.94$--$0.99$.

At the low-A/P Ancipa, SWOT at first appeared to diverge sharply from the gauge. This traced
to three specific dates (2024-10-18, 2024-11-08, 2024-11-29) where SWOT read a nearly-constant
${\sim}920$--$924$~m while the gauge, and independently the SAR-measured water area, both
showed a real, continuous drawdown toward the reservoir's minimum (SAR area falling to
$0.26$~ha by mid-November, essentially dry). This is not a gauge or offset problem: SWOT's Prior
Lake detection evidently failed to track the water body once it shrank to a sliver, and the raw
SWOT record has no observations at all for the following five months (2024-11-29 to
2025-04-24) --- a genuine gap in the product itself, not one introduced by our cleaning.
Excluding these three dates, gauge and SWOT agree almost perfectly ($r=1.00$,
Table~\ref{tab:wlrmse}): SWOT is reliable at Ancipa away from its most extreme drawdown, a
materially different and more precise conclusion than a blanket claim that SWOT degrades at
low A/P would suggest, though the underlying mechanism --- detection failure once the exposed
water body becomes very small --- is itself an A/P-adjacent limitation, consistent with the
degradation \citet{PaperOne} documents for SAR retrieval in the same geometric regime. Olivo,
Castello and Nicoletti also have usable SWOT records, once matched to each reservoir's own
footprint rather than its granule's metadata, reported above. DAHITI is not evaluated
separately: for the reservoirs where it exists it is a reprocessed
SWOT product, matching SWOT epochs at $>96\%$ of dates to within $\pm0.6$~m.

Four of the nine gauges have a documented reliability problem in the publicly available
record we rely on (Section~\ref{sec:data}), a limitation of what is accessible to us rather
than necessarily of the water authority's own internal instrumentation; each is resolved with
the same substitution logic rather than by discarding the affected dates: Rosamarina's sensor
froze at two different flat readings across
$\sim$19 months combined; Garcia's sensor reads the dry lakebed floor instead of the true level
whenever the reservoir empties below $\sim$176~m, a fault that recurred twice a year apart
(2024-09-04 to 2025-02-13 and 2025-08-06 to 2026-02-03); Nicoletti's sensor was inoperative
until 2023-11 and then, once it began reporting, frozen at a single flat value for a further 14
months (2023-11-15 to 2025-01-17). Nicoletti's gauge-SWOT offset also shifts across the record,
from a tight $-1.89$~m (2025-02 to 2026-02) to $-0.2$ to $-0.6$~m from 2026-03 on; the cause is
unexplained, and we leave it as a disclosed limitation rather than a second correction. Pozzillo's
sensor combines both patterns: a 13-month frozen episode (2023-08-27 to 2024-10-03) sits on top
of a persistent range-compression across the rest of the record, caught only because an
independent signal (SAR water area) was checked against both candidate water levels rather than
the gauge being trusted by default. Gauge and SWOT correlate almost perfectly ($r=1.00$) once
Pozzillo's frozen episode is excluded; the remaining KGE gap is a pure variance mismatch
($\alpha=1.62$), and the SAR area corroborates SWOT independently, correlating more strongly
with SWOT ($r=0.90$) than with the gauge ($r=0.76$) over the same period. The gauge under-represents
Pozzillo's true range; this is not a SWOT error. Rosamarina's, Garcia's and Nicoletti's are
intermittent malfunctions with a clear onset and recovery; Pozzillo's range-compression is a
persistent, whole-record limitation layered on top of its own frozen episode. In
each case SWOT (Rosamarina, Garcia, Pozzillo) fills the gap for the affected reconstruction
dates; Nicoletti's own selected reconstruction dates happen to all postdate its gauge's
recovery, so its freeze affects the water-level validation above but not its reconstruction,
which the boletin-derived fallback already covers for dates before the gauge existed at all
(Section~\ref{sec:data}). That four of nine gauges needed this kind of substitution in
practice, not merely in principle, is itself evidence for pairing SAR with a gauge-independent
altimetry source rather than relying on in-situ networks alone. What this water-level
substitution does to reconstructed \emph{volume}, once it is carried through the
reconstruction, is a question about the reconstruction's output, not the input, and is
addressed together with the other output validations in Section~\ref{sec:res_wlvol}.

\subsection{Reconstructed bathymetry and capacity change}
\label{sec:res_change}
With both inputs validated, the waterline-stacked DEMs reconstruct the exposed basin of all
nine reservoirs over the 2022--2026 window, yielding 2D/3D bathymetry and a capacity-change
estimate against each reservoir's design curve (Table~\ref{tab:capacity}, Fig.~\ref{fig:change}).
Pozzillo's DEM is built from SWOT rather than the gauge, per Section~\ref{sec:res_inputvalid}; every other
reservoir uses the gauge (substituting SWOT or the design curve only inside a documented
malfunction window, Section~\ref{sec:res_inputvalid}). Relative to the design curve, the
SAR-observable (band) capacity change ranges from a modest $-4.2\%$ (Pozzillo) to $-46.1\%$
(Olivo). The exposed band itself represents a widely varying share of each reservoir's total
design-curve volume, $38.8\%$--$92.0\%$ (mean $71\%$ across the eight reservoirs for which
this ratio is well posed) --- so for most reservoirs the majority of storage is directly
observed, but the fraction is far from uniform and is smallest exactly where A/P is lowest.
Ancipa is excluded from this ratio: its design curve's lowest tabulated quota sits above the
corrected DEM's true floor, so the raw ratio exceeds 100\% (a design-curve extrapolation
artifact, not a real deep zone); we report it as $100\%$ with this caveat rather than force a
spurious number.

The apparent band loss itself tracks A/P: the three lowest-A/P reservoirs (Olivo $50.7$~m,
Ancipa $90.5$~m, Nicoletti $119.7$~m) show the largest losses ($-46.1\%$, $-41.0\%$,
$-39.3\%$), while the two highest-A/P reservoirs (Poma $190.1$~m, Pozzillo $240.5$~m) show the
smallest ($-12.8\%$, $-4.2\%$). Fig.~\ref{fig:aevgrid} shows why directly: at low A/P
(Olivo, Nicoletti) the reconstructed volume--elevation curve sits well to the left of
\emph{both} the design and the updated curve at every level, not just at the top, which is
the signature of an under-reconstructed DEM rather than a curve that has genuinely moved: a
real capacity loss would leave the updated curve near the design curve while only the SAR
curve shifts, whereas here the SAR curve alone is displaced from both. At medium/high A/P
(Garcia, Arancio, Rosamarina, Poma, Pozzillo) the SAR curve instead tracks the updated
curve closely across most of the range. Section~\ref{sec:res_curves} quantifies this pattern
directly; Section~\ref{sec:res_unc} asks how much of it is a correctable bias.

\subsection{Validation against independent references}
\label{sec:res_valid}

\subsubsection{Field validation against an echo-sounder survey (Garcia)}
\label{sec:res_garcia}
Against the December-2025 echo-sounder survey, the Garcia DEM has an overall bias of
$-0.88$~m and RMSE $2.67$~m in the SAR-observable range ($\mathrm{R}^2=0.61$). A residual
elevation dependence remains, from $-1.62$~m near the floor ($175$--$176$~m) to $-2.43$~m near
the top ($189$--$190$~m) (Fig.~\ref{fig:garcia}). At full pool, the survey reveals a
$14.29$~\si{Mm^3} storage loss versus design ($27\%$ of design capacity), of which the SAR DEM
detects $10.91$~\si{Mm^3} ($21\%$) --- the SAR estimate captures $76\%$ of the surveyed loss,
directly: a $3.4$~\si{Mm^3} shortfall against the field-measured total, consistent with the
DEM's own negative bias above.

\subsubsection{Validation against recent bathymetric curves}
\label{sec:res_curves}
Six reservoirs have a recent official area--volume curve as a secondary reference
(Table~\ref{tab:capacity}, rightmost column; Fig.~\ref{fig:aevgrid}). The comparison is
informative less for the raw size of any single gap than for its pattern across A/P. At high
A/P, SAR and the official curve agree closely: Poma's official curve shows essentially no net
loss ($-0.5\%$ band, $+2.9\%$ total), while the SAR DEM shows a modest $-12.8\%$ band deficit
attributable to the low-elevation reconstruction bias characterised in Section~\ref{sec:res_unc}
rather than real sedimentation --- a useful negative-control case. Rosamarina and Arancio show
a similar pattern at a smaller offset (SAR $-13.3\%$/$-15.7\%$ versus survey/curve
$-7.3\%$/$-3.6\%$ band). At low A/P the gap widens sharply: Castello ($-30.0\%$ vs.\
$-23.5\%$), Olivo ($-46.1\%$ vs.\ $-3.6\%$) and Nicoletti ($-39.3\%$ vs.\ $-6.3\%$) all show
SAR over-estimating the true band loss by a wide margin, Olivo and Nicoletti most severely.
This is the same A/P-ordered pattern already visible in Section~\ref{sec:res_change}:
reconstruction reliability, not just apparent capacity change, degrades as A/P falls.

\subsubsection{Volume-level validation of the gauge-SWOT substitution}
\label{sec:res_wlvol}
Section~\ref{sec:res_inputvalid} validated SWOT as a water level on its own terms; we now
ask what carrying that substitution through the reconstruction does to \emph{storage}.
Keeping the SAR area fixed, the in-situ gauge and SWOT altimetry (both offset-corrected,
Section~\ref{sec:res_inputvalid}) are paired separately with the same area series, fitted to
the same power-law hypsometry, and the resulting band storages compared to each other and to
field/survey truth over the common gauge$\cap$SWOT elevation band. Across the four
reservoirs with an independent truth (Garcia, Poma, Rosamarina, Arancio), SWOT storage
differs from truth by a mean of $2.1$~\si{Mm^3} (Garcia $+0.5$, Poma $-5.2$, Rosamarina
$-2.0$, Arancio $-0.7$~\si{Mm^3}), somewhat larger than gauge-based storage's own mean error
against truth ($1.8$~\si{Mm^3} over the same bands). The gap is driven mainly by Poma:
correcting SWOT's offset there raises its fitted range enough that the common band is now
bounded by the gauge's own higher ceiling rather than SWOT's (previously artificially low)
one, reaching further into the reservoir's range than the earlier, offset-uncorrected band did
--- a part of the curve where neither satellite-paired reconstruction tracks the official
curve as closely; Garcia, by contrast, now agrees almost exactly, reflecting its own
gauge-record fix (Section~\ref{sec:res_inputvalid}). For the two reservoirs without a survey,
gauge and SWOT storage differ by $+4.2$~\si{Mm^3} at Pozzillo --- the clearest volume-level
signature yet of its persistent range-compression (Section~\ref{sec:res_inputvalid}), not a
malfunction --- and by only $+0.3$~\si{Mm^3} at Ancipa, a close agreement once its own three
bad SWOT dates are excluded (Section~\ref{sec:res_ladder}), in contrast to the much larger gap
that same substitution would have shown beforehand.

Beyond storage volume, the spatial reconstruction itself agrees just as closely: rebuilding
Poma's DEM with every mask's level inferred purely from SWOT, with no gauge value used
anywhere in the substitution, reproduces the gauge-based DEM to $0.31$~m RMSE ($0.06$~m bias)
over the entire basin, with no pixel differing by more than $1$~m.

\subsubsection{Cross-sensor validation: PlanetScope optical reconstruction}
\label{sec:res_xsensor}
Reconstructing the bathymetry from PlanetScope 3~m optical masks (Section~\ref{sec:xsensor};
7--58 masks per reservoir) and comparing to the Sentinel-1 DEM, the two independent sensors
agree closely across the observable band at medium/high A/P: the per-pixel elevation RMSE is
$1.25$~m (Pozzillo, A/P~240), $1.65$~m (Poma, A/P~190) and $1.61$~m (Rosamarina, A/P~187) ---
of the same order as the Garcia \emph{field} RMSE ($2.67$~m) (Fig.~\ref{fig:planet}). At
Pozzillo, the optical masks are paired with SWOT rather than the gauge
(Section~\ref{sec:res_inputvalid}), for consistency with its primary reconstruction and, with
both series offset-corrected and cleaned of the same confirmed bad dates
(Section~\ref{sec:res_inputvalid}), a further, independent corroboration on top of the
SAR-area evidence that already motivated the SWOT-over-gauge choice there. Expressed as total
band volume, the optical and SAR reconstructions differ by $2.9$~\si{Mm^3} at
\emph{Pozzillo} ($23.5$ vs $20.6$) and $3.1$~\si{Mm^3} at \emph{Rosamarina} ($35.4$ vs $38.6$)
--- consistent with, not larger than, the per-pixel RMSE integrated over each reservoir's
observable area --- giving these two reservoirs, which lack a modern field survey, an
independent near-truth on the same footing as Garcia's echo-sounder.

Rosamarina's and Pozzillo's comparison bands are each narrower than their full
SAR-reconstructed range, but no longer for the same reason. New PlanetScope acquisitions from
the second half of 2025 into 2026 (Section~\ref{sec:data}) close what was previously an
archive gap at the \emph{top} of Rosamarina's range: the optical band now reaches
$162.4$~m, matching the SAR reconstruction's own $162.3$~m ceiling; a modest gap remains
instead at the \emph{bottom} ($145.1$ vs.\ the SAR floor at $140.6$~m), where even the
earliest available PlanetScope acquisitions do not reach quite as low as the deepest
SAR-observed drawdown. Pozzillo received no new acquisitions in this round: its optical masks
(once paired to SWOT) still reach only $350.3$~m, a real $\sim$$4.6$~m gap against the SAR
reconstruction's own $354.9$~m ceiling. Rosamarina also illustrates why the water-level source
matters within its shared window: its in-situ gauge was stuck for $\sim$19 months across the
2024--2026 window (Section~\ref{sec:res_inputvalid}); substituting SWOT --- which, like the
optical masks, records the real drawdown --- restores a physical bathymetry and the reported
cross-sensor agreement. The two reconstructions diverge sharply (RMSE $8.50$~m) only at
\emph{Ancipa} (A/P~90); tellingly, this divergence \emph{grew}, rather than shrank, once two
new PlanetScope acquisitions (2024-11-14, 2024-11-25) were added specifically to close a
gap in its archive that fell inside the SAR reconstruction's own drawdown window
(2024-05-24 to 2024-12-19) --- the RMSE was $6.06$~m with 5 masks, before these two dates were
available. Extending the optical record deeper into Ancipa's low-A/P, narrow basin did not
resolve the mismatch; it sharpened it, indicating the divergence is a genuine, geometry-driven
reconstruction limit at low A/P rather than an artifact of a temporal sampling gap that more
data would fix. Because optical NDWI and SAR backscatter fail under
orthogonal conditions, agreement between them elsewhere is strong evidence the reconstruction
is not dominated by sensor-specific error.

\subsubsection{Practical validation: reconstructed volume against official monitoring records}
\label{sec:res_operational}
Beyond validating the reconstruction itself, we test whether it improves day-to-day reservoir
management. Each reservoir's calibration mask/level pairs give a smooth area--elevation curve
by the same power-law hypsometric fit already used to densify the reconstruction
(Section~\ref{sec:dem}), rather than the pixel-count DEM raster directly: with only around ten
calibration masks, the level-slice DEM has real, physically implausible steps in area(h)
between calibration dates, invisible in the volume--elevation view of Fig.~\ref{fig:aevgrid}
(volume is area's integral, so the same steps only show there as a faint kink) but serious
once area is queried pointwise at hundreds of time-series dates, as here. Applying this smooth
curve to each reservoir's full, multi-year Sentinel-1 area time series converts area directly
to a volume time series, which we compare against the water authority's own officially
registered volume record (monthly, 2007--2026; daily, 2023--2026 where available), independent
of any curve used in this study. The same conversion is repeated with the original design
curve, so both are tested exactly the same way. This is possible now for the five reservoirs
with a full area record (Ancipa, Poma, Pozzillo, Rosamarina, Garcia); the other four (Arancio,
Castello, Olivo, Nicoletti) have only the calibration masks used to build the reconstruction
itself, not a standalone time series, and are left for a future extension.

The reconstructed curve only covers the area range the drawdown-exposed band itself observed,
so applying it to an area outside that range is extrapolation, not a genuine estimate;
comparisons are restricted to dates within this range, which covers $30$--$90\%$ of each
reservoir's record depending on how much of its historical range the 2022--2026 window
actually traversed, narrowest at Ancipa, whose low A/P bounds how much of the basin the
drawdown ever exposed (Section~\ref{sec:res_change}).

\subsection{Uncertainty}
\label{sec:res_unc}
One question drives this section: when the SAR band capacity change (Table~\ref{tab:capacity})
disagrees with an independent reference, how much of that gap is a systematic, correctable
bias, and how much is left over once the correction is applied? Garcia is the only reservoir
with a genuine field survey, so it is the only place the reconstruction's own error can be
measured directly rather than inferred: its per-band error runs from $-1.62$~m near the floor to
$-2.43$~m near the top (Section~\ref{sec:res_garcia}), a near-linear trend we fit as
bias$(f) = +2.22 - 3.66f$~m, $f$ the fractional height in the exposed band. Applying this same
correction to Rosamarina and Poma --- the two other reservoirs with an independent volume
reference, excluding Garcia itself --- lowers their mean absolute band-capacity-change error
from $9.2\%$ (uncorrected) to $7.9\%$ (corrected): a real but partial improvement, not a fix.
Fig.~\ref{fig:uncertainty} shows this concretely: panel 2 plots the raw SAR estimate, the
corrected estimate, and the independent truth side by side for each reservoir, and the
corrected bars move toward truth without reaching it. We separately checked whether this
modest correction is itself well constrained --- resampling the bias line's two fitted
coefficients from their covariance moves the corrected capacity-change estimate by only
$\pm1.3\%$ (median, panel 3) --- so the residual error is not fitting noise; the correction is
precise, it is simply incomplete. The $\pm8.5$ percentage-point RMS of the \emph{corrected}
errors at Rosamarina and Poma (Garcia excluded as the self-calibration case) is accordingly the
number to budget for in practice, not the $\pm1.3\%$ calibration figure.

This residual error does not, by itself, explain the much larger low-A/P discrepancies in
Section~\ref{sec:res_curves} (Olivo $-46.1\%$ vs.\ $-3.6\%$; Nicoletti $-39.3\%$ vs.\
$-6.3\%$): those gaps are several times larger than $\pm8.5$ points. We read them instead as
a genuine A/P-driven reconstruction-reliability ceiling, the same mechanism already
established for SAR area retrieval itself \citep{PaperOne}, rather than a version of the same
correctable bias measured at Garcia. In short: a few metres of elevation bias, correctable to
about $\pm8.5$ percentage points of band capacity, is the uncertainty budget for reservoirs in
Garcia's A/P range and above; below it, the dominant error is not this bias but the A/P
ceiling itself, and no bias correction fitted here removes it.
