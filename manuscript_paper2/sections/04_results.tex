\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Validation of the SAR area and water-level inputs}
\label{sec:res_inputvalid}
The Sentinel-1 water-surface-area input is not re-validated here: its reliability, and its
dependence on shoreline area-to-perimeter ratio (A/P), is established in \citet{PaperOne}
and simply applied (Section~\ref{sec:inputvalid}).

\label{sec:res_ladder}
The water level is the other required input, and where a gauge is unavailable it must itself
be remotely sensed. We validate the substitute strictly \emph{as a water level} here, with no
SAR area or reconstruction involved yet. SWOT is first cleaned with the same outlier-removal
pipeline as \citet{PaperOne} (global $2\sigma$ filter, then local $(5,1.5\sigma)$ applied
twice and $(10,1.5\sigma)$ once), and any date falling inside a reservoir's documented
gauge-malfunction window (Section~\ref{sec:data}) is matched against SWOT instead of the raw
gauge reading, so neither series is compared against known-bad data. A per-reservoir constant
offset (SWOT minus gauge, estimated once from the clean overlap) is then removed from SWOT:
SWOT's WSE and a gauge reading are not guaranteed to share a vertical datum, and one
reservoir (Poma) shows the textbook signature of a pure datum mismatch rather than a real
water-level disagreement (correlation $r=1.00$ before correction, with the mean offset
alone accounting for essentially all of the raw RMSE). This is the same treatment already
applied to the boletin-derived water level elsewhere in the pipeline (Section~\ref{sec:data}),
now extended to SWOT; it changes RMSE, which is sensitive to an absolute offset, but leaves
KGE (whose mean-ratio term is small relative to these reservoirs' absolute elevations)
essentially unaffected. Fig.~\ref{fig:wlsource}
plots the cleaned SWOT against the gauge over the full SWOT era (2023-07 onward) for all nine
reservoirs, and Table~\ref{tab:wlrmse} summarises the agreement as both an RMSE and a KGE
(the same skill score used throughout \citet{PaperOne}, so the two studies' notions of
``agreement'' are directly comparable). We call a source \emph{acceptable} when its error is
small relative to the reconstruction's own elevation bias (Section~\ref{sec:res_unc}, a few
metres in amplitude, KGE $\geq0.8$ once cleaned): six of nine reservoirs clear this bar
(Arancio, Poma, Garcia, Rosamarina, Olivo, Castello, RMSE $0.16$--$0.75$~m, KGE
$0.80$--$0.99$).

At the low-A/P Ancipa, SWOT visibly diverges from the gauge (Fig.~\ref{fig:wlsource}) and the
RMSE jumps an order of magnitude ($8.81$~m even after the datum correction); unlike Pozzillo, nothing here suggests the gauge
is at fault, so we read this as a genuine SWOT limitation at low A/P, consistent with the
degradation \citet{PaperOne} documents for SAR itself in the same geometric regime. Olivo,
Castello and Nicoletti's SWOT records were initially missed by a metadata (bounding-box)
search of SWOT Prior Lake granules, which over-matched and returned only unrelated lakes
elsewhere in the same swath; the correct prior-lake identifiers, found instead from the PLD
vector product itself (spatially matched to each reservoir's actual footprint, not its
granule's bounding box), reveal real, usable SWOT records for all three, reported above.
DAHITI is not evaluated separately: for the reservoirs where it exists it is a reprocessed
SWOT product, matching SWOT epochs at $>96\%$ of dates to within $\pm0.6$~m.

Four of the nine gauges used elsewhere in this study have a documented reliability problem,
each resolved with the same substitution logic rather than by discarding the affected dates
(Section~\ref{sec:data}): Rosamarina's sensor froze at two different flat readings across
$\sim$19 months combined; Garcia's sensor reads the dry lakebed floor instead of the true level
whenever the reservoir empties below $\sim$176~m; Nicoletti's sensor was inoperative until
2023-11 and then, once it began reporting, frozen at a single flat value for a further 14
months (2023-11-15 to 2025-01-17); and Pozzillo's sensor, while never frozen, under-represents
the reservoir's true water-level range throughout the record (footnote, Table~\ref{tab:wlrmse}).
The first three are intermittent malfunctions with a clear onset and recovery; Pozzillo's is a
persistent, whole-record limitation, caught only because an independent signal (SAR area) was
checked against both candidate water levels rather than the gauge being trusted by default. In
each case SWOT (Rosamarina, Garcia, Pozzillo) fills the gap for the affected reconstruction
dates; Nicoletti's own selected reconstruction dates happen to all postdate its gauge's
recovery, so its freeze affects the water-level validation above but not its reconstruction,
which the boletin-derived fallback already covers for dates before the gauge existed at all
(Section~\ref{sec:data}). That four of nine gauges needed this kind of substitution in
practice, not merely in principle, is itself evidence for pairing SAR with a gauge-independent
altimetry source rather than relying on in-situ networks alone. What this water-level
substitution does to reconstructed \emph{volume}, once it is carried through the
reconstruction, is a question about the reconstruction's output, not the input, and is
addressed together with the other output validations in Section~\ref{sec:res_wlvol}.

\subsection{Reconstructed bathymetry and capacity change}
\label{sec:res_change}
With both inputs validated, the waterline-stacked DEMs reconstruct the exposed basin of all
nine reservoirs over Periods A (2014--2016) and B (2022--2026), yielding 2D/3D bathymetry and
A-vs-B change maps (Table~\ref{tab:capacity}, Fig.~\ref{fig:change}). Pozzillo's Period-B DEM
is built from SWOT rather than the gauge, per Section~\ref{sec:res_inputvalid}; every other
reservoir uses the gauge (substituting SWOT or the design curve only inside a documented
malfunction window, Section~\ref{sec:res_inputvalid}). Relative to the design curve, the
SAR-observable (band) capacity change ranges from a modest $-3.0\%$ (Pozzillo) to $-47.3\%$
(Olivo). The exposed band itself represents a widely varying share of each reservoir's total
design-curve volume, $39.1\%$--$87.2\%$ (mean $70\%$ across the eight reservoirs for which
this ratio is well posed) --- so for most reservoirs the majority of storage is directly
observed, but the fraction is far from uniform and is smallest exactly where A/P is lowest.
Ancipa is excluded from this ratio: its design curve's lowest tabulated quota sits above the
corrected DEM's true floor, so the raw ratio exceeds 100\% (a design-curve extrapolation
artifact, not a real deep zone); we report it as $100\%$ with this caveat rather than force a
spurious number. Nicoletti's Period-A and Period-B water-level ranges share only a narrow
overlap ($\sim$12\% of change-map pixels), so its capacity-change estimate rests on a
correspondingly narrow elevation window and should be read with that caveat.

The apparent band loss itself tracks A/P: the three lowest-A/P reservoirs (Olivo $50.7$~m,
Ancipa $90.5$~m, Nicoletti $119.7$~m) show the largest losses ($-47.3\%$, $-41.0\%$,
$-40.2\%$), while the two highest-A/P reservoirs (Poma $190.1$~m, Pozzillo $240.5$~m) show the
smallest ($-14.7\%$, $-3.0\%$). Fig.~\ref{fig:aevgrid} shows why directly: at low A/P
(Olivo, Nicoletti) the reconstructed volume--elevation curve sits well to the left of
\emph{both} the design and the updated curve at every level, not just at the top, which is
the signature of an under-reconstructed DEM rather than a curve that has genuinely moved: a
real capacity loss would leave the updated curve near the design curve while only the SAR
curve shifts, whereas here the SAR curve alone is displaced from both. At medium/high A/P
(Garcia, Arancio, Rosamarina, Poma, Pozzillo) the SAR curve instead tracks the updated
curve closely across most of the range. Section~\ref{sec:res_curves} quantifies this pattern
directly; Section~\ref{sec:res_unc} asks how much of it is a correctable bias.

\subsection{Validation against independent references}
\label{sec:res_valid}

\subsubsection{Field validation against an echo-sounder survey (Garcia)}
\label{sec:res_garcia}
Against the December-2025 echo-sounder survey, the Garcia DEM has an overall bias of
$+0.25$~m and RMSE $2.53$~m in the SAR-observable range ($\mathrm{R}^2=0.64$). A residual
elevation dependence remains, from $+0.3$~m near the floor ($175$--$176$~m) to $-2.1$~m near
the top ($189$--$190$~m) (Fig.~\ref{fig:garcia}). At full pool, the survey reveals a
$13.02$~\si{Mm^3} storage loss versus design ($26\%$ of design capacity), of which the SAR DEM
detects $13.21$~\si{Mm^3} ($26\%$) --- the SAR estimate matches the surveyed loss almost
exactly, capturing $101\%$ of it.

\subsubsection{Validation against recent bathymetric curves}
\label{sec:res_curves}
Six reservoirs have a recent official area--volume curve as a secondary reference
(Table~\ref{tab:capacity}, rightmost column; Fig.~\ref{fig:aevgrid}). The comparison is
informative less for the raw size of any single gap than for its pattern across A/P. At high
A/P, SAR and the official curve agree closely: Poma's official curve shows essentially no net
loss ($-0.5\%$ band, $+2.9\%$ total), while the SAR DEM shows a modest $-14.7\%$ band deficit
attributable to the low-elevation reconstruction bias characterised in Section~\ref{sec:res_unc}
rather than real sedimentation --- a useful negative-control case. Rosamarina and Arancio show
a similar pattern at a smaller offset (SAR $-14.8\%$/$-17.3\%$ versus survey/curve
$-7.3\%$/$-3.7\%$ band). At low A/P the gap widens sharply: Castello ($-31.2\%$ vs.\
$-23.4\%$), Olivo ($-47.3\%$ vs.\ $-3.6\%$) and Nicoletti ($-40.2\%$ vs.\ $-6.2\%$) all show
SAR over-estimating the true band loss by a wide margin, Olivo and Nicoletti most severely.
This is the same A/P-ordered pattern already visible in Section~\ref{sec:res_change}:
reconstruction reliability, not just apparent capacity change, degrades as A/P falls.

\subsubsection{Volume-level validation of the gauge-SWOT substitution}
\label{sec:res_wlvol}
Section~\ref{sec:res_inputvalid} validated SWOT as a water level on its own terms; we now
ask what carrying that substitution through the reconstruction does to \emph{storage}.
Keeping the SAR area fixed, the in-situ gauge and SWOT altimetry (both datum-corrected,
Section~\ref{sec:res_inputvalid}) are paired separately with the same area series, fitted to
the same power-law hypsometry, and the resulting band storages compared to each other and to
field/survey truth over the common gauge$\cap$SWOT elevation band. Across the four
reservoirs with an independent truth (Garcia, Poma, Rosamarina, Arancio), SWOT storage
differs from truth by a mean of $2.4$~\si{Mm^3} (Garcia $-1.4$, Poma $-5.3$, Rosamarina
$-2.1$, Arancio $-0.7$~\si{Mm^3}), somewhat larger than gauge-based storage's own mean error
against truth ($1.6$~\si{Mm^3} over the same bands). The gap is driven mainly by Poma:
correcting SWOT's datum there raises its fitted range enough that the common band is now
bounded by the gauge's own higher ceiling rather than SWOT's (previously artificially low)
one, reaching further into the reservoir's range than the earlier, datum-uncorrected band did
--- a part of the curve where neither satellite-paired reconstruction tracks the official
curve as closely. For the two reservoirs without a survey, gauge and SWOT storage now differ
by $-1.4$~\si{Mm^3} at Pozzillo (a modest, reservoir-consistent difference, not a
malfunction) and by $+1.8$~\si{Mm^3} at Ancipa --- the same low-A/P reservoir already
flagged as unreliable at the water-level stage, not a new failure mode introduced by the
volume integration.

\subsubsection{Cross-sensor validation: PlanetScope optical reconstruction}
\label{sec:res_xsensor}
Reconstructing the bathymetry from PlanetScope 3~m optical masks (Section~\ref{sec:xsensor};
7--58 masks per reservoir) and comparing to the Sentinel-1 DEM, the two independent sensors
agree closely across the observable band at medium/high A/P: the per-pixel elevation RMSE is
$1.58$~m (Pozzillo, A/P~240), $1.89$~m (Poma, A/P~190) and $1.84$~m (Rosamarina, A/P~187) ---
of the same order as the Garcia \emph{field} RMSE ($2.53$~m) (Fig.~\ref{fig:planet}). At
Pozzillo, the optical masks are paired with SWOT rather than the gauge
(Section~\ref{sec:res_inputvalid}), for consistency with its primary reconstruction; once
both series are datum-corrected (Section~\ref{sec:res_inputvalid}), this specific
pixel-comparison check no longer discriminates between the two (SWOT-paired bias
$+0.89$~m/RMSE $1.58$~m vs.\ gauge-paired $+0.56$~m/$1.54$~m) --- the case for SWOT at
Pozzillo rests on the independent SAR-area evidence, not on this cross-sensor check.
Expressed as total band volume, the optical and SAR reconstructions differ by $3.6$~\si{Mm^3}
at \emph{Pozzillo} ($24.5$ vs $28.2$) and $2.7$~\si{Mm^3} at \emph{Rosamarina} ($35.7$ vs
$38.4$) --- consistent with, not larger than, the per-pixel RMSE integrated over each
reservoir's observable area --- giving these two reservoirs, which lack a modern field
survey, an independent near-truth on the same footing as Garcia's echo-sounder.

Rosamarina's and Pozzillo's comparison bands are each narrower than their full
SAR-reconstructed range, but no longer for the same reason. New PlanetScope acquisitions from
the second half of 2025 into 2026 (Section~\ref{sec:data}) close what was previously an
archive gap at the \emph{top} of Rosamarina's range: the optical band now reaches
$162.4$~m, matching the SAR reconstruction's own $162.3$~m ceiling; a modest gap remains
instead at the \emph{bottom} ($145.1$ vs.\ the SAR floor at $140.5$~m), where even the
earliest available PlanetScope acquisitions do not reach quite as low as the deepest
SAR-observed drawdown. Pozzillo received no new acquisitions in this round: its optical masks
(once paired to SWOT) still reach only $352.7$~m, a real $\sim$$3.7$~m gap against the SAR
reconstruction's own $356.4$~m ceiling. Rosamarina also illustrates why the water-level source
matters within its shared window: its in-situ gauge was stuck for $\sim$19 months across the
2024--2026 window (Section~\ref{sec:res_inputvalid}); substituting SWOT --- which, like the
optical masks, records the real drawdown --- restores a physical bathymetry and the reported
cross-sensor agreement. The two reconstructions diverge sharply (RMSE $8.50$~m) only at
\emph{Ancipa} (A/P~90); tellingly, this divergence \emph{grew}, rather than shrank, once two
new PlanetScope acquisitions (2024-11-14, 2024-11-25) were added specifically to close a
gap in its archive that fell inside the SAR reconstruction's own drawdown window
(2024-05-24 to 2024-12-19) --- the RMSE was $6.06$~m with 5 masks, before these two dates were
available. Extending the optical record deeper into Ancipa's low-A/P, narrow basin did not
resolve the mismatch; it sharpened it, indicating the divergence is a genuine, geometry-driven
reconstruction limit at low A/P rather than an artifact of a temporal sampling gap that more
data would fix. Because optical NDWI and SAR backscatter fail under
orthogonal conditions, agreement between them elsewhere is strong evidence the reconstruction
is not dominated by sensor-specific error.

\subsection{Uncertainty}
\label{sec:res_unc}
One question drives this section: when the SAR band capacity change (Table~\ref{tab:capacity})
disagrees with an independent reference, how much of that gap is a systematic, correctable
bias, and how much is left over once the correction is applied? Garcia is the only reservoir
with a genuine field survey, so it is the only place the reconstruction's own error can be
measured directly rather than inferred: its per-band error runs from $+0.3$~m near the floor to
$-2.1$~m near the top (Section~\ref{sec:res_garcia}), a near-linear trend we fit as
bias$(f) = +2.30 - 3.74f$~m, $f$ the fractional height in the exposed band. Applying this same
correction to Rosamarina and Poma --- the two other reservoirs with an independent volume
reference, excluding Garcia itself --- lowers their mean absolute band-capacity-change error
from $7.3\%$ (uncorrected) to $6.7\%$ (corrected): a real but partial improvement, not a fix.
Fig.~\ref{fig:uncertainty} shows this concretely: panel 2 plots the raw SAR estimate, the
corrected estimate, and the independent truth side by side for each reservoir, and the
corrected bars move toward truth without reaching it. We separately checked whether this
modest correction is itself well constrained --- resampling the bias line's two fitted
coefficients from their covariance moves the corrected capacity-change estimate by only
$\pm1.1\%$ (median, panel 3) --- so the residual error is not fitting noise; the correction is
precise, it is simply incomplete. The $\pm10.3$ percentage-point RMS of the \emph{corrected}
errors at Rosamarina and Poma (Garcia excluded as the self-calibration case) is accordingly the
number to budget for in practice, not the $\pm1.1\%$ calibration figure.

This residual error does not, by itself, explain the much larger low-A/P discrepancies in
Section~\ref{sec:res_curves} (Olivo $-47.3\%$ vs.\ $-3.6\%$; Nicoletti $-40.2\%$ vs.\
$-6.2\%$): those gaps are several times larger than $\pm10.3$ points. We read them instead as
a genuine A/P-driven reconstruction-reliability ceiling, the same mechanism already
established for SAR area retrieval itself \citep{PaperOne}, rather than a version of the same
correctable bias measured at Garcia. In short: a few metres of elevation bias, correctable to
about $\pm10$ percentage points of band capacity, is the uncertainty budget for reservoirs in
Garcia's A/P range and above; below it, the dominant error is not this bias but the A/P
ceiling itself, and no bias correction fitted here removes it.
