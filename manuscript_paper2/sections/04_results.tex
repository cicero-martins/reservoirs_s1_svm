\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Reconstructed bathymetry and capacity change}
\label{sec:res_change}
The waterline-stacked DEMs reconstruct the exposed basin of all five core reservoirs
over Periods A (2014--2016) and B (2022--2026), yielding 2D/3D bathymetry and A-vs-B
change maps. Relative to the 1960s design curve, the SAR-observable (band) capacity
change is Ancipa $-36\%$, Pozzillo $-18\%$, Garcia $-23\%$, Poma $-15\%$ and
Rosamarina $-14\%$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:change}). Where a modern survey exists, the
\emph{total} loss can be larger (Rosamarina $-17\%$; the deep zone, invisible to SAR,
carries the balance), confirming that the SAR band estimate is a lower bound.

\begin{figure}[htbp]\centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{bathymetry_change_summary.png}
  \caption{SAR-detected capacity change versus the 1960s design curve for the five
  core reservoirs (band-relative), with independent survey change and, where
  available, the total loss including the deep zone. Ordered by A/P.}
  \label{fig:change}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Field validation against an echo-sounder survey (Garcia)}
\label{sec:res_garcia}
Against the December-2025 echo-sounder survey, the Garcia DEM has an overall bias of
$-0.43$~m and RMSE $2.2$~m in the SAR-observable range. A residual elevation
dependence remains, from $+1.7$~m near the floor to $-2.3$~m near the top
(Fig.~\ref{fig:garcia}). The survey reveals a $24.3\%$ storage loss versus design at
full pool, of which the SAR DEM recovers $87\%$ --- the band capacity change
($-23\%$) closely matching the surveyed loss.

\begin{figure}[htbp]\centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{garcia_validation_deep.png}
  \caption{Deepened Garcia validation: (left) error stratified by elevation band;
  (centre) volume--elevation curves; (right) sedimentation-loss capture.}
  \label{fig:garcia}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Validation against recent bathymetric curves}
\label{sec:res_curves}
For Rosamarina the SAR DEM matches the 2025 survey curve to within $+0.6$~\si{Mm^3}
in the observable band and reproduces the real capacity loss; for Poma, whose updated
survey shows essentially no net loss, the SAR shows a small ($\sim$10\%) apparent
deficit attributable to the low-elevation reconstruction bias (a cautionary case);
for Arancio the scalar hypsometry matches the 2022 survey curve with an area RMSE of
$10.7$~ha ($R^2=0.96$). % [TODO: optional per-reservoir curve figures]

\subsection{Fully remote storage: gauge versus SWOT}
\label{sec:res_ladder}
Keeping the SAR area and reconstruction fixed and swapping only the water-level
source, the in-situ gauge and SWOT altimetry give band storages that agree with each
other and with the survey truth. Across the four survey-validated reservoirs, SWOT
storage differs from field truth by a mean of $2.2$~\si{Mm^3} (Garcia $-3.5$, Poma
$-0.7$, Rosamarina $-4.1$, Arancio $-0.7$~\si{Mm^3}), with SWOT--gauge water-level
RMSE of $0.8$--$3.7$~m (Fig.~\ref{fig:ladder}). For the two reservoirs without a
survey, gauge and SWOT storage agree to $-0.3$~\si{Mm^3} (Pozzillo) but diverge at
low A/P (Ancipa, $-3.2$~\si{Mm^3}, water-level RMSE $12$~m).

We find that DAHITI is not an independent source for these reservoirs: its records
begin exactly at the SWOT launch (2023-07) and match SWOT epochs at $>96\%$ of dates
with a water-level offset of only $+0.2$ to $-0.6$~m, i.e.\ DAHITI is a reprocessed
SWOT product. The gauge-free result therefore rests on gauge-versus-SWOT.

  \caption{Water-level substitution: in-situ gauge versus SWOT swath altimetry (SAR
  area identical), with survey truth where available. DAHITI, a reprocessed-SWOT
  product, is not shown.}
  \label{fig:ladder}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Scalability and the A/P control}
\label{sec:res_ext}
Extending the scalar method to additional Sicilian reservoirs, the fit quality tracks
the drawdown amplitude and the A/P: wide-drawdown, higher-A/P reservoirs (Arancio,
Castello) reconstruct well ($R^2\approx0.97$), while a low-A/P reservoir (Olivo,
A/P~51~m) is poorly constrained ($R^2=0.14$), reproducing in the volume domain the
area-monitoring reliability pattern of \citet{PaperOne} (Fig.~\ref{fig:extended}).

  \caption{Extended scalar hypsometry against updated survey curves; reconstruction
  quality scales with drawdown amplitude and A/P.}

\end{figure}

\subsection{Cross-sensor validation: PlanetScope optical reconstruction}
\label{sec:res_xsensor}
Reconstructing the bathymetry from PlanetScope 3~m optical masks
(Section~\ref{sec:xsensor}; 5--39 masks per reservoir) and comparing to the Sentinel-1
DEM, the two independent sensors agree closely across the observable band: the per-pixel
elevation RMSE is $1.1$~m (Pozzillo, A/P~240), $1.3$~m (Poma, A/P~190) and $2.6$~m
(Rosamarina, A/P~187) --- of the order of the Garcia \emph{field} RMSE ($2.2$~m)
(Fig.~\ref{fig:planet}). The agreement is strongest precisely where no modern field
survey exists: the optical and SAR band storages match to $0.2$~\si{Mm^3} at
\emph{Pozzillo} ($37.9$ vs $38.0$) and to $0.4$~\si{Mm^3} at \emph{Rosamarina}
($9.6$ vs $10.1$), giving these reservoirs an independent near-truth. Rosamarina also
illustrates why the water-level source matters: its in-situ gauge was stuck at
$145.72$~m to the centimetre for ${\sim}10$ months of the 2024--2025 optical window,
collapsing the level-slice to a single flat elevation; substituting SWOT --- which, like
the optical masks, records the real ${\sim}17$~m drawdown --- restores a physical
bathymetry and the $2.6$~m cross-sensor agreement. The two reconstructions diverge
(RMSE $5.5$~m) only at \emph{Ancipa} (A/P~90), where the narrow, low-A/P geometry
degrades both the optical masks and the SWOT water level --- the same A/P limit
identified throughout. Because optical NDWI and SAR backscatter fail under orthogonal
conditions, agreement between them is strong evidence the reconstruction is not dominated
by sensor-specific error.

\begin{figure}[htbp]\centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{planet_vs_sar.png}
  \caption{Cross-sensor check: area--elevation curves from the PlanetScope (optical,
  3~m) and Sentinel-1 (SAR) bathymetric reconstructions, over the common observable
  band. Close agreement at medium/high A/P (Pozzillo, Rosamarina, Poma); divergence at
  low A/P (Ancipa).}
  \label{fig:planet}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Uncertainty}
\label{sec:res_unc}
The Garcia-derived normalised bias model transfers only partially --- it is consistent
with Rosamarina but not with the anomalous Poma --- so the dominant, systematic transfer
uncertainty on the band capacity change is ${\approx}\pm10$ percentage points; the
random per-pixel component averages out ($\pm0.6\%$) (Fig.~\ref{fig:uncertainty}).
